Seawater · Structure · Systems

Water, energy and food, engineered as one system.

Tidal Frame Systems is developing integrated systems that pair solar-powered desalination with controlled-environment agriculture, guided by environmental sensing and data. Our aim is practical: dependable freshwater and food production in places where both are under strain.

The problem

  • Freshwater is limited where it is needed most.Many coastal and arid communities sit beside water they cannot drink or farm with.
  • Food production depends on that water.When supply is unreliable, so are harvests.
  • The systems are built separately.Water, energy and agriculture are usually designed and run apart, which wastes all three.

What we are building.

Exploring

Solar-Powered Freshwater Production

We are designing compact desalination and water-treatment units that run on solar power, for sites without dependable grid or municipal supply. The goal is a decentralized source of freshwater whose output and quality are measured continuously.

Next milestoneA bench unit completes a continuous multi-day run with logged water output, energy use per unit of water, and product-water conductivity.

In development

Controlled-Environment Food Production

We are developing hydroponic growing systems that use treated water and monitored nutrient delivery to grow crops with less water and steadier yields. The intent is dependable local food where soil, rainfall or climate make field farming unreliable.

Next milestoneOne full crop cycle is completed with recorded water use and yield, compared against a cited published baseline.

In development

Environmental Sensing and Decision Support

We are building a sensing and data layer that tracks water quality, energy and growing conditions across a system in real time. Operators get early warning and decision support first; automated control follows only when the data justifies it.

Next milestoneA sensor node logs at least 30 days of continuous data (for example conductivity, temperature, pH, flow) and a sample dataset is published.

Approach

How the parts connect

  1. Measure. Sensors track water quality, energy and growing conditions continuously.

  2. Produce. Solar power drives desalination and treatment; the water feeds controlled-environment crops.

  3. Coordinate. Data from every stage informs how the whole system is sized and run.

Coastal and freshwater intelligence

Desalination starts at the shoreline. Salinity, temperature, turbidity and algal activity in the source water determine how a system performs and how long it lasts, so we treat source-water monitoring as a core part of the engineering and not an add-on. The same measurements are useful beyond our own systems: to aquaculture operators, to water managers, and to anyone tracking coastal and freshwater quality. We intend to share what we measure as our monitoring work matures.

Long-term concept: from land to water

We are starting small, on land, and building toward the water.

Beyond the four phases above, we imagine floating, sun-powered platforms that turn seawater into freshwater, and freshwater into harvests, right beside the coastal communities that need them most.

About

Tidal Frame Systems

Tidal Frame Systems is an engineering company working on food security, freshwater availability and resource-efficient agriculture. We combine systems engineering, renewable energy, and environmental sensing to design infrastructure that is practical to build and maintain. We are based in San Diego, California and are at an early stage: our focus now is technical development, testing, and finding the right research and pilot partners.
